What This System Was Built For
This is healthcare provider fraud detection — CPT codes, E&M levels, CMS billing rules, provider peer comparison, upcoding rings. That's health insurance payer work. Think Anthem, UnitedHealthcare, Humana, CMS/Medicare.

What Prudential Actually Does
Prudential is primarily life insurance, disability, annuities, and group benefits. Their fraud landscape is completely different.

Prudential's Actual Fraud Types
Life Insurance Fraud:

Misrepresentation on applications (hiding medical history, smoking status)
Stranger-originated life insurance (STOLI)
Staged deaths or fake death certificates
Beneficiary fraud (forged documents, impersonation)
Premium diversion by agents
Disability Claims Fraud (probably their biggest SIU workload):

Claimant says they can't work but they're running a business
Exaggerated symptoms that don't match surveillance
Friendly doctors writing indefinite disability letters
Claimant working under the table while collecting benefits
Group Benefits Fraud:

Employees adding fake dependents
Employers misclassifying workers to reduce premiums
Dental/vision claims for services not rendered (if Prudential administers those)
Agent/Producer Fraud:

Churning policies for commissions
Forging signatures on applications
Misappropriating premium payments
Selling unauthorized products
How a Prudential Fraud Analyst Actually Works
For disability claims (their bread and butter):

1. Claim comes in — claimant says they can't work due to 
   back injury

2. Claims examiner reviews:
   - Medical records from treating physician
   - Independent medical exam results
   - Functional capacity evaluation
   - Employer's job description (what physical demands?)
   - Prior claim history with Prudential

3. Red flags that trigger SIU referral:
   - Claim filed right after policy purchase
   - Claimant has history of multiple disability claims
   - Medical documentation is thin or from a single provider
   - Social media shows claimant doing activities inconsistent 
     with claimed disability
   - Claimant is uncooperative with IME scheduling

4. SIU investigation:
   - Surveillance (literally watching the claimant)
   - Social media analysis (Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn)
   - Database checks (are they employed elsewhere?)
   - Interviews with claimant, employer, treating physician
   - Financial analysis (did their spending change?)
For life insurance fraud:

1. Death claim filed

2. Claims examiner checks:
   - Policy age (claims within contestability period get scrutiny)
   - Cause of death vs what was disclosed on application
   - Beneficiary relationship to insured
   - Multiple policies across carriers (MIB database check)

3. Red flags:
   - Large policy purchased recently
   - Insurable interest questions
   - Death certificate from unfamiliar jurisdiction
   - Beneficiary changed recently
   - Application medical history doesn't match death cause
What's Completely Different From Healthcare Fraud
Healthcare Fraud	Prudential Fraud
Provider is the bad actor	Claimant or agent is the bad actor
High volume statistical patterns	Individual case investigation
CPT codes and billing data	Medical records and surveillance
Peer comparison across providers	Claimant behavior analysis
Network analysis for rings	Social media and public records
Automated detection at scale	Human judgment on individual claims
What Would Actually Help a Prudential Analyst
For disability claims:

NLP that reads medical records and summarizes functional limitations
Social media monitoring that flags activity inconsistent with claimed disability
Pattern matching across their book (same doctor supporting multiple suspicious claims)
Timeline analysis (when was policy purchased vs when was claim filed vs when did condition allegedly start)
Predictive scoring on new claims (likelihood of fraud based on claim characteristics)
For life insurance:

Application-to-claims comparison (automated check of what was disclosed vs cause of death)
MIB and prescription database cross-referencing
Contestability period alert system
Beneficiary change pattern detection
For agent fraud:

Lapse/replace ratio monitoring by agent
Signature verification
Premium payment tracking
Customer complaint correlation
Bottom Line
The system in your logs is a healthcare payer fraud tool. If Prudential is your target, you'd need to redesign around:

Individual claimant investigation instead of provider profiling
Document analysis (medical records, death certificates, applications) instead of billing pattern statistics
Behavioral indicators (social media, surveillance, employment checks) instead of CPT code analysis
Policy lifecycle analysis (when purchased, when claimed, contestability) instead of peer comparison
The agentic architecture could still work but the tools, data sources, and investigation logic would be entirely different. The workflow planning approach I described earlier still applies — just swap out every healthcare-specific element for the equivalent Prudential use case.

If you're building this for Prudential specifically, you need a Prudential SIU analyst in the room from day one. Not a healthcare fraud SME. Different world.
